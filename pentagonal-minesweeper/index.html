Ruleset

Asking once marks what to look at; asking again names the reasoning.

Click to uncover. Right-click cycles a flag, then a ghost flag, then a ghost ring, then bare again — the ghosts are for sketching a supposition's what-ifs, and the counters never count them. Clicking a number that already has its full quota of flags beside it uncovers everything else it touches.

Under a ruleset, a flag's pennant answers for the law: red where the flags as placed already satisfy it, amber where they do not yet — a pair unpaired, a group unjoined, a chain shy of the rim. Under the Two Groups law each connected company of flags wears its own colour instead, the six largest distinct and the rest grey. When every mine is accounted for, a button appears to clear the rest of the board in one press.

Uncovering a mine shows it for a moment and covers it again; the board is never lost, only given up on. A board finished without touching a mine and without asking for a hint sets a best time — any other is played for its own sake.

Difficulty

Each level stands on a ladder of reasoning. Easy asks only one-glance rules — counting, full groups, boxes read like numbers. Medium hides some of the numbers and asks two-fact rules besides: overlaps, bottlenecks, sealed pockets. Hard hides every number the board can spare and hunts for boards that need as many different kinds of reasoning as it can find — crossed numbers, budgets, the endgame against the mine counter — so a large one takes a few seconds to deal. Harder deals only boards the ordinary rules cannot finish at all: somewhere a supposition must be granted and followed to a contradiction, and the hint will walk that chain with you step by step. A cell that never shows its count still proves itself safe, and every board on every level can be finished without guessing.

Strict

With this on, uncovering a cell that the board does not yet rule out as a mine finds a mine there. Only cells you can actually prove safe are safe, so guessing never pays. Every board can still be finished, and a clean run played this way sets a best time like any other — a strict pseudo-mine counts as a mine touched.

Show neighbours

With this on, resting the pointer on a number lights the cells it counts — which a tiling of pentagons does not make obvious by looking.

Loose box clues

With this on, a box that would hand over its whole contents — a nought, or a box needing every cell it has left — may say “at least” or “at most” instead of its exact count, so its ground stays covered and has to be reasoned for. Only the box laws are touched.
